[ 1 · WHAT IT IS ]

You already own a gaming PC. Jochona gets you to it from the couch, the desk, or a handheld: it streams video and audio down, and sends your controller back, over your own network.

 ┌──────────────┐                                       ┌──────────────┐
 │ YOUR DEVICE  │ ◂══ VIDEO · H.264 / HEVC / AV1 ══════ │ YOUR HOST PC │
 │              │ ◂══ AUDIO · stereo / surround ═══════ │              │
 │ couch · desk │ ══▸ INPUT · pad / kb / mouse ════════▸ │ Sunshine     │
 │ · handheld   │                                       │ Apollo       │
 └──────────────┘   your LAN, or your private overlay   │ Vibepollo    │
                                                        └──────────────┘
 ┌─────────────────┐
 │   YOUR DEVICE   │
 └───────┬─────────┘
   video ▴ audio ▴
   input ▾
   LAN · overlay
 ┌───────┴─────────┐
 │  YOUR HOST PC   │
 │ Sunshine·Apollo │
 │    Vibepollo    │
 └─────────────────┘

On the LAN the path is direct. Through a private overlay like Tailscale it stays end-to-end encrypted. Either way it never touches a Jochona server — we didn't build one, on purpose.

Host features are capability-aware: Jochona surfaces what your host actually reports, and nothing it doesn't.

[ 3 · CONTROLLER ]

Every ordinary workflow is designed to finish without touching a keyboard:

> pair with a host
PIN on the TV, confirmed with the pad
> browse your games
D-pad-first navigation, deterministic focus
> launch and play
in-session overlay for volume, stats, disconnect
> drop and resume
fast reconnect after Wi-Fi blips or suspend

A keyboard and mouse stay welcome, low-latency, and first-class. They're just never required.

[ 4 · LOCAL-FIRST ]

Your pairings stay on your device, and credentials go in the vault your operating system already guards. Never a config file, never plain text.

A unified library across your hosts, per-game streaming profiles, and the certification target: 4K 120 Hz HDR with AV1, where the complete hardware path supports it.

POST #48 · PLANNED FOR LATER

A self-hosted companion service for remote wake and trusted-friend access, plus an optional self-hosted relay for when a direct path fails. All of it open source. A Jochona account never becomes mandatory.
