Your games are already on your PC.

Jochona gets you to them from the couch, the desk, or a Steam Deck. The stream runs over your own network, and nothing sits in the middle that you don't own. It's a streaming client for the Sunshine, Apollo, and Vibepollo hosts you already run.

The whole route is yours.

There are only three pieces: the screen in your hands, the network in your house, and the PC doing the heavy lifting. Jochona keeps them talking directly and stays out of the way.

DEVICE → NETWORK → HOST

Your device

Windows, macOS, Linux, a Steam Deck, or a Windows handheld like the ROG Ally. Everything works from a controller on the couch; a keyboard is welcome, never required.

DESK · COUCH · HANDHELD

Your network

Stay on your LAN, or reach home through Tailscale, NetBird, or ZeroTier. The stream itself is happy on any of them; discovery and wake are choosier. The path table below shows exactly what each route carries.

LAN · TAILSCALE · NETBIRD · ZEROTIER

Your host

Sunshine, Apollo, or Vibepollo. Jochona asks the host what it can actually do and shows you those controls, instead of guessing from a version number.

CAPABILITY-AWARE

Every road home works. They carry different things.

Streaming is just packets between two machines you own, so it survives any route. Finding hosts and waking them are pickier, because they depend on what the network can carry.

LAN · OVERLAY · INTERNET

What each network path supports
PathStreamingFinding hostsWaking a sleeping PC
Same LAN Direct. The lowest-latency path there is. Automatic. Hosts announce themselves over mDNS. Yes. Wake-on-LAN magic packets just work.
Tailscale · NetBird Great. An encrypted WireGuard tunnel, straight to your host. Manual entry. The tunnel doesn't carry the multicast that discovery listens for. No. These are layer-3 networks, and the wake packet is a layer-2 broadcast. Beacon exists to close exactly this gap.
ZeroTier Great. Encrypted, and direct wherever the network allows. Works. The overlay behaves like a LAN, broadcast included. With setup. A sleeping PC drops off the overlay, so the packet needs a bridge you configure on the LAN side. Beacon does the same job without the bridging homework.
Direct internet Works when you deliberately open a path to your host. Manual entry. No. Nothing along the way knows how to deliver a broadcast.

SLIDE FOR THE FULL TABLE →

One overlay gotcha Jochona plans for: pairing over Tailscale can cache the tunnel's MAC address instead of your PC's real one, which quietly breaks wake even at home. The stored MAC stays visible and editable.

It isn't that nothing sits in the middle. It's that you can own all of it.

Rented clouds solve real problems: waking a distant PC, identity, friends, awkward NATs. Jochona solves the same problems with pieces you host yourself. The client exists today; the rest is designed and named, and every piece will be open source.

CLIENT · HOST · CONSTELLATION · BEACON

  • Jochona Client

    IN DEVELOPMENT · CI BUILDS

    The screen side, and the software this site is about. Controller-first streaming for the Sunshine, Apollo, and Vibepollo hosts you already run.

  • Jochona Host

    IN DEVELOPMENT

    A Sunshine-derived host for the PC doing the work. Baseline GameStream stays untouched, so any Moonlight-family client still connects.

  • Jochona Constellation

    PLANNED

    The whole management plane, named for what it does: it arranges every machine you can stream to into one picture. Identity, trusted friends, host presence, and wake; home PCs and rented gaming VMs as a single fleet, honest about where each machine stands: start or wake requested, machine booting, host reachable, streaming ready. Constellation starts a cloud VM when you sit down and shuts it down when you leave, so idle hours cost nothing, and friends only ever launch on terms you approve. When no direct path exists, it can stand up a relay where it's hosted: bandwidth-governed, observable, and never able to grant access on its own.

  • Jochona Beacon

    PLANNED

    A small always-on box on your home LAN, and the network's town crier. Overlays like Tailscale can't carry the wake broadcast from far away, so Beacon sends the magic packet for you, watches your host come up, and announces what it sees; a Stream Deck panel shows your fleet and wakes a host at a keypress.

Nothing in the middle you don't own.

Today that promise is easy to audit: there is no Jochona account, relay, or server your stream could detour through, because none exist yet. When Constellation and Beacon arrive, they run on your hardware, under your rules.

Your pairings stay on your device. The bar for 1.0 puts credentials in the vault your operating system already guards, not a config file, not plain text.

HOST IDENTITY CHANGED

If your host changes, Jochona stops and asks.

A reinstalled host, or a different machine answering at the same address, is blocked until you deliberately re-pair. Nothing gets trusted quietly.

Where this is headed.

By 1.0

One library across every host you pair, per-game streaming profiles, and certified end-to-end paths for 4K 120 Hz HDR with AV1 where the whole hardware chain supports it.

Planned for later

The rest of the lineup. Constellation brings remote wake, trusted-friend access, cloud-VM fleets, and a last-resort relay for the rare path that can't go direct; Beacon wakes hosts your overlay can't reach and puts your fleet on a Stream Deck. All of it stays open source and self-hostable, and a Jochona account never becomes mandatory.
